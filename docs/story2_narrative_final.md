# Story 2: What Is Happening in Windsor's Rental Market? (Final Narrative)

**Source:** CMHC Rental Market Survey, Windsor CMA, October 2020 to October 2025. Purpose-built rental buildings with 3 or more units, apartments and row houses combined unless stated. ## Introduction

Windsor has 19,883 purpose-built rental units. Each October, CMHC surveys them for rent, vacancy and how often tenants move. This story follows those measures over six surveys, from 2020 to 2025, and across the area's seven rental zones, to show how the market has changed and where it is tightest.

## Rents rose every year

The average rent across all unit sizes went from **$953 a month in 2020 to $1,293 in 2025**, an increase of **35.7%** in five years. Rent went up in every one of those years, with the largest jumps in 2021 (10.9%) and 2024 (8.1%).

Two-bedroom units rose the most: from $1,038 to **$1,451**, up $413 or **39.8%**. Studios rose 35.8% to $948, and one-bedroom units 32.9% to $1,193. Three-bedroom units are the exception: CMHC's figures show their rent falling in 2023 and 2024, but the sample is small, the early years are graded "d" (use with caution), and no 2025 figure is published, so that series is the least certain.

## Vacancy dipped, then recovered

Vacancy did not move in a straight line. It was 3.5% in 2020, fell to a low of **1.8% in 2022**, when very few units were free, and then climbed back to **3.6% in 2025**. A rate around 3% is often taken as a balanced market, so Windsor has returned to roughly that point.

Rents kept rising through the recovery: as more units became available in 2024 and 2025, rents still went up 8.1% and 5.9%. More vacancy has not yet meant lower rents. In 2025, studios have the most room (5.9% vacant) and two- and three-bedroom units the least (3.1%).

## Where renters live, and what they pay

Most rental housing is in the city. The four Windsor city zones hold 82% of all units; Zone 1, the Centre, alone has 5,509 (28%). The Centre is also the cheapest zone, at **$1,155** a month, with the highest vacancy (4.6%) and one of the highest turnover rates (13.3%).

The county zones are smaller, pricier and tighter. Amherstburg has only 597 units but the highest average rent, **$1,955**, which is $800 more than the Centre. North Essex County ($1,682) is second. Vacancy in the three county zones is between 2.0% and 2.7%, below every city zone except East Outer.

## Who moves

About 1 in 8 units (11.9%) changed tenants in the year to October 2025. Turnover is highest for studios (15.0%) and lowest for three-bedroom units (5.3%): households in larger units, often families, move far less often, which also keeps fewer large units on the market at any time.

## What this means

Windsor's rental market has loosened since 2022, but rents have kept climbing faster than in earlier years. Story 1 found the median renter household earned $48,400 in 2020; at 30% of income, that affords about $1,210 a month, already below the 2025 average rent of $1,293. Story 3 looks at whether new construction is adding the kind of units renters need, and Story 4 at how these rents compare with similar cities. CMHC's zone tables cannot be downloaded before 2021, so Story 4 also gives the longer view: Windsor's rent and vacancy every October since 2015, comparing the five years before the pandemic with the five from it.

## Limitations

- The survey covers purpose-built rentals only; rented condos, basements and houses are not included.
- Rent is the average for all surveyed units, occupied and vacant, not the asking rent for a vacant unit, so new tenants usually pay more.
- CMHC suppresses some zone and unit-size figures, and grades the rest by reliability; small segments such as three-bedroom units are less certain.
- Rents are not adjusted for inflation.
