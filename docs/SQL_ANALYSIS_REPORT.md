## Limitations

- Story 1 is a single Census year (2021). The source extract only holds the Total housing-suitability / Total dwelling-condition slice, and 971 of the 3,360 possible combinations are absent; absent rows are not created. StatCan rounds counts to multiples of 5, so components can differ slightly from totals.
- Story 1: for income bands of $10,000 and over, the missing 'Not applicable' count is taken as 0 by definition (Not applicable = household income ≤ 0) and flagged `zero_by_definition`. Elsewhere a missing 'Not applicable' row gives a NULL rate (`rate_null_reason`), e.g. owner/renter rows of household types 7–13.
- Story 1: in 4 very small 'Under $10,000' cells, random rounding makes the 30%+ count exceed the applicable households; the rate is left NULL (`numerator_exceeds_denominator_rounding`), never capped, with numerator and denominator still shown.
- Story 1: '100% or more' is derived (30%+ minus 30% to <100%), not a published category.
- Story 2 covers only the two survey periods in the workbook (Oct-24, Oct-25) and the two CMHC rent-change periods. Many cells are suppressed by CMHC (`**`), especially for row/townhouse and small zones; those stay NULL and are excluded from every calculation, so some comparisons are unavailable.
- Story 2: `change_value` for average rent compares two published averages from different samples; CMHC's same-sample rent change (`story2_same_sample_rent_change`) is the like-for-like measure.
- Story 3 is one monthly edition (August 2026, plus January–August 2026 year to date and under-construction stock at month end). No trend, no annualisation, no Ontario/Canada values.
- Story 4 is apartment-only (CMHC Table 1.0) and compares Windsor only with the national table's own Windsor row, never with Windsor `row_and_apartment` figures. Ottawa-Gatineau appears as two parts (Ont. / Qué.), each ranked as listed in the source. Rent growth has suppressed / not-significant values (NULL) for some CMAs, so fewer CMAs are ranked for that metric. No comparator subset is selected.
- No affordability-gap index is computed: renter-income growth data is not in the validated sources.
- Views are descriptive. They do not support causal conclusions.
