## Introduction

Rents and affordability (Stories 1 and 2) depend partly on how many new homes are built. CMHC counts every home whose construction starts and finishes in the Windsor area each month. This story follows those counts from 2016 to 2025 to see how much Windsor is building, and what kind of housing it is.

## How much is being built

Windsor started between about 950 and 1,550 homes a year from 2016 to 2023, an average of roughly 1,300. In **2024** starts jumped to **2,157**, the most in the ten years, or 13.0 for every 1,000 households. The jump did not last: **2025 fell to 1,262 starts, 41% fewer**, back to the level of 2016. In the first nine months of 2026, 690 homes were started.

Over the full ten years Windsor started 13,905 homes and completed 13,113.

## What kind of housing

The mix has changed more than the total. In 2016 only **6%** of the homes started were apartments; singles made up most of the rest (804 of 1,266). By 2021 apartments were 42% of starts, and in **2025 they were 60%** (752 of 1,262). Single-detached starts fell from 804 in 2016 to 331 in 2025.

This matters for renters: purpose-built rentals are mostly apartments, so the shift points toward more of the housing type renters use. Not every apartment start is a rental, though; CMHC's "apartment and other" category also includes condominiums.

## Finishing versus starting

A home is counted again when it is finished, usually one to three years after it starts. In **2023 and 2025 Windsor completed more homes than it started** (1,967 against 1,208, and 1,671 against 1,262), as earlier projects reached the end. The number of homes under construction peaked at about 2,000 in 2022 and again at the end of 2024, and was 1,635 at the end of 2025. With fewer starts feeding it, the pipeline is getting smaller.

## What this means

Windsor is building more apartments than it used to, but not more homes overall: the 2024 surge was a single year, and 2025 returned to the long-run level. Story 2 found vacancy rising since 2022, and the high completions of 2023 to 2025 are consistent with that. With starts down again and fewer homes under construction, the flow of newly finished homes is likely to slow over the next few years unless starts recover.

For context, in the same quarter (April to June 2026) apartments were 55.5% of Windsor's starts but 70.5% of Ontario's. Windsor's shift toward apartments is large, but it still builds a higher share of houses than the province as a whole.

## Limitations

- Starts and completions are counted for the whole Windsor area; they are not split by rental zone or by whether the homes are for rent or for sale.
- "Per 1,000 households" uses the 2021 Census household count for every year, so it shows the trend, not an exact rate.
- 2026 covers January to September only.
