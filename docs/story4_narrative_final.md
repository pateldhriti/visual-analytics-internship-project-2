## Introduction

A rent of $1,454 means little on its own: is that high or low? This story places Windsor beside four Ontario cities of similar size: London, Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo, Hamilton and St. Catharines-Niagara. These were chosen because they are close to Windsor in population and CMHC publishes the same tables for them; Toronto and Ottawa are several times larger and were left out. All five are compared on the same three measures, for the same unit type and the same month.

## Windsor is the cheapest of the five

In October 2025 the average 2-bedroom apartment in Windsor rented for **$1,454 a month**, the lowest of the five cities. St. Catharines-Niagara was next at $1,527, then London ($1,651) and Hamilton ($1,656), with Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo the most expensive at $1,832. Windsor sits **$199.50 below the five-city median** of $1,653.50, and $378 below Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo. It was also the cheapest a year earlier, at $1,387 in October 2024.

## Cheap, but not loose

Lower rent does not mean more empty apartments. Windsor's apartment vacancy rate was **3.7%**, the second lowest of the five; only Hamilton was tighter, at 3.6%. London (4.0%), St. Catharines-Niagara (3.9%) and Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo (4.1%) all had more vacant units.

Vacancy rose in all five cities between 2024 and 2025: Windsor's went from 3.3% to 3.7%, and London's from 2.9% to 4.0%. So Windsor's market loosened along with the rest of the region, not on its own.

## Rents are rising at a middle pace

For apartments that were in the survey both years, CMHC reports that Windsor's 2-bedroom rent rose **3.6%** from October 2024 to October 2025. That places Windsor third of five: faster than Hamilton (1.6%) and Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo (3.3%), slower than London (4.1%) and St. Catharines-Niagara (5.5%). Using the same buildings in both years keeps new, more expensive buildings from inflating the figure.

## The closest match is pulling further ahead

St. Catharines-Niagara is Windsor's closest peer on price: its 2-bedroom rent was only $73 a month higher in October 2025. It also had the fastest rent growth of the five, 5.5% against Windsor's 3.6%. If those rates continued, the gap between the two would widen rather than close, so Windsor's position as the cheapest market is not under immediate pressure from its nearest rival. Hamilton shows the opposite pattern: the tightest vacancy of the five, yet the slowest rent growth (1.6%), so a tight market does not always mean fast-rising rents.

## Tenants in Windsor move least

Windsor also had the **lowest turnover** of the five: 12.2% of apartments changed tenants in the year, against a five-city median of 14.1%. Fewer moves means fewer units come up for rent at any one time, which fits with Windsor's below-median vacancy.

## What this means

Windsor is the least expensive of these five comparable Ontario markets, but it is not the easiest place to find an apartment: vacancy is below the group median and tenants move less often. Being cheaper than its peers also does not make Windsor affordable for its own renters. Story 1 found the median renter household in Windsor earned $48,400 in 2020; at 30% of income that covers about $1,210 a month, well under the $1,454 average 2-bedroom rent shown here.

## Limitations

- Apartments only. Story 2's Windsor figures include row houses, so they differ slightly (for example 3.6% vacancy instead of 3.7%).
- Five cities, not a national ranking. The comparison shows where Windsor stands among similar markets, not among all of Canada.
- The affordability-gap index (rent growth minus renter income growth) cannot be computed: none of the project's sources reports renters' income year by year, so it is not estimated.
- Average rent includes long-time tenants, so it is lower than what a new tenant would pay.
